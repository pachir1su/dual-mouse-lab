# Academic Papers and HCI Research

## 1. Bimanual Manipulation in a Curve Editing Task

- Authors: Russell Owen, Gordon Kurtenbach, George Fitzmaurice, Thomas Baudel, Bill Buxton
- Year: 1998
- Topic: two-handed manipulation, task integration, divided attention
- Accessible copy: https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=231c965ed727581ec5a76f13fb4d5249eeb1c6fc&repid=rep1&type=pdf

Why it matters: the paper explicitly discusses the general design of two input devices such as two mice, one per hand, with two cursors. It also argues that bimanual input must be designed carefully; simply doubling independent tasks can create coordination and attention costs.

## 2. Towards Seamless Support of Natural Collaborative Interactions

- Authors: Stacey D. Scott, Garth B. D. Shoemaker, Kori M. Inkpen
- Venue: Graphics Interface 2000
- Year: 2000
- Paper: https://graphicsinterface.org/wp-content/uploads/gi2000-15.pdf

The study compared three puzzle-solving conditions: physical paper, one mouse/one cursor, and two mice/two cursors. The two-cursor setup supported simultaneous independent interaction by two children. It is a key historical reference for multi-mouse/multi-cursor systems.

## 3. A Symmetric Interaction Model for Bimanual Input

- Author: Celine Latulipe
- Institution: University of Waterloo
- Type: PhD thesis
- Year: 2006
- PDF: https://uwspace.uwaterloo.ca/bitstream/handle/10012/2915/clatulip2006.pdf

This thesis develops and evaluates symmetric bimanual interaction techniques. It is useful for deciding when both hands should contribute equally to the same task instead of assigning completely separate jobs to the two cursors.

## Research questions for this repository

- When is two-cursor input faster or more expressive than one cursor?
- How much attention-switching cost comes from tracking two distant pointers?
- Should the left and right mouse have different sensitivity?
- Are symmetric tasks easier to learn than asymmetric tasks?
- Which derived two-pointer gestures are reliable enough for games?
- How should onboarding teach the non-dominant hand?
